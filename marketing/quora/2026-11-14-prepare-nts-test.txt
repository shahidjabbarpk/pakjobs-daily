NTS (and similar testing services like OTS, PTS, ETEA) papers for government jobs follow a fairly predictable pattern, so a focused plan works better than reading random books. Here is a method that works for most candidates:

1. Read the advertisement and the test details first
Every NTS project has its own page with the post, the test pattern and the syllabus. Most job tests are MCQ-based and usually cover English, General Knowledge / Pakistan Affairs, Islamiat, Basic Maths / Analytical reasoning, and sometimes subject-specific questions for the post. Note the number of questions, time and whether there is negative marking.

2. Build the basics, subject by subject
- English: synonyms/antonyms, correct the sentence, prepositions, tenses, short comprehension.
- General Knowledge & Pakistan Studies: history of Pakistan, constitution basics, geography, current affairs of the last 6–12 months.
- Islamiat: basic Quran and Hadith facts, Islamic history.
- Maths/Analytical: percentages, ratios, averages, simple equations, series and logical reasoning.
- Subject part: revise your degree's core topics if the post asks for it.

3. Solve past papers and MCQ books under time
Past papers show which topics repeat. Solve full papers with a timer – speed matters as much as knowledge, because you usually get less than a minute per question.

4. Daily routine
1–2 hours a day for 3–4 weeks is enough for most job tests if you are consistent: one subject for theory, then 30–50 MCQs, then check mistakes and keep a small "mistakes notebook".

5. On test day
Carry your roll number slip and original CNIC, reach early, attempt easy questions first, and do not guess blindly if there is negative marking.

6. Stay away from fake "guaranteed pass" offers
No one can sell you the paper or a seat. Download roll number slips and results only from the official testing service website.
