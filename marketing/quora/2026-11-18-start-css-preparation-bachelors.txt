Q: How do I start CSS (civil services of Pakistan) preparation from the beginning? I am currently doing a bachelor's.

Starting while you are still in your bachelor's is actually a big advantage. You have time to build the two things that decide most CSS results: strong English writing and a habit of reading. Here is a simple way to begin.

1. Read the official scheme first. Download the current CSS rules, syllabus and past papers from the FPSC website. The exam has an MCQ screening test (MPT), then the written exam (compulsory papers plus optional subjects), then medical, psychological assessment and the viva. FPSC updates the rules from time to time, so always rely on the official site, not on old blog posts.

2. Make English your daily subject. Most candidates fail in the English Essay and Precis papers, not in optional subjects. Write one essay every week on a current topic, keep it organised (outline, arguments, conclusion) and get it checked by someone strict. Practise precis and comprehension from past papers.

3. Build a reading habit. Read one good English newspaper daily and make short notes on Pakistan's economy, foreign policy, governance and climate issues. These notes will later serve Current Affairs, Pakistan Affairs and the essay.

4. Choose optional subjects carefully. Pick subjects you understand and can write long answers in, ideally related to your degree, instead of only following "high scoring" lists. Look at past papers of each option before deciding.

5. Prepare for the screening test early. Do MCQs of English, general ability, Pakistan affairs, Islamic studies and current affairs a little every day.

6. Plan realistically. Many successful candidates prepare for a year or more. While you are studying, 1–2 hours a day is enough to build the base; increase it after graduation.

7. Keep other doors open. Alongside CSS, apply for FPSC, PPSC and other government posts that match your degree. Interviews and tests for those jobs are good practice too.
