Question: How do I prepare for the lower division clerk LDC written test in Pakistan MCS?

LDC (Lower Division Clerk) tests in Pakistan follow a fairly similar pattern across departments and institutions, so you can prepare in a structured way even if the exact paper is not published. First, read the advertisement carefully: it tells you the testing body, the age limit, the required education and whether there is a typing test. The exact pass marks and typing speed are written there, so confirm them from the official ad.

What is usually asked in the written test (MCQs):
- General knowledge and Pakistan Studies: history of Pakistan, geography, constitution basics and current affairs.
- Islamiat (or Ethics for non-Muslim candidates).
- English: grammar, synonyms/antonyms, correct the sentence, sometimes writing an application or letter.
- Urdu: grammar, meanings and idioms.
- Basic maths: percentages, averages, ratios and simple word problems.
- Computer basics: MS Word, Excel, keyboard shortcuts, email and internet terms.

A simple 6-week plan:
- Weeks 1–2: Pakistan Studies, Islamiat and GK for 1–2 hours a day. Make short notes you can revise quickly.
- Weeks 3–4: English and Urdu grammar, plus about 20 maths questions daily.
- Weeks 5–6: computer basics, past papers and timed mock tests. Re-do every question you got wrong.
- Every day: 20–30 minutes of typing practice, because many clerk posts also have a typing test and speed builds slowly.

Other tips:
- Keep your documents (CNIC, domicile, certificates, photos) attested and ready before applying.
- Practise under time pressure; most candidates lose marks by running out of time, not from lack of knowledge.
- Never pay anyone who promises a "guaranteed" clerk job. Selection is only through the official test and merit.
